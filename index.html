What am I looking at?

A status page for an automated crypto trading experiment. It refreshes on a schedule, so it is a recent snapshot rather than a live ticker.

  • The money is fake. This trades on Binance's practice network with a pretend balance and real prices. No real funds are involved anywhere, and the code refuses to run against a real account.
  • The rule it trades with ('momentum') has never been proven to work. It is running to test that the plumbing — placing orders, handling fills, enforcing the safety limits — actually functions. It is not running because there is evidence it makes money.
  • So profit here means nothing. If this page shows a gain, it is luck over a tiny number of trades, not a discovery. Every term used below is explained in plain English at the bottom of the page.

Every term on this page, in plain English

No finance background assumed. If a word above was unclear, it is defined here.

Testnet (paper trading)
Practice mode. Binance gives you a pretend account with fake money and real prices. Nothing here is real money, and nothing here can become real money.
Equity
The total value of the account right now — cash plus whatever open bets are currently worth. This is the one number that says how you are doing overall.
Position
A bet that is currently open. You have a position in a coin until you close it.
Long
A bet that the price will go UP. You make money if it rises.
Short
A bet that the price will go DOWN. You make money if it falls.
Entry price
The price it bought (or sold) at when it opened the bet.
Mark price
What that coin is worth right now, used to value an open bet.
P&L (profit and loss)
How much money was made or lost. A positive number is a gain, negative is a loss.
Unrealised P&L
Profit or loss on a bet that is still open — it is on paper and can still vanish. It only becomes real when the position closes.
Realised P&L
Profit or loss that is locked in because the position was closed. This one cannot change.
Closed trade (round trip)
One complete open-then-close cycle. Buying and later selling the same coin is one closed trade. Only closed trades have a final profit or loss.
Win rate
Out of the finished trades, the percentage that made money. 60% means 6 in 10 made a profit. On its own it means little — losing big on the other 4 still loses overall.
Expectancy
The average profit per finished trade, wins and losses combined. This is the number that actually compounds. Positive means the average trade makes money.
Profit factor
Total money won divided by total money lost. Above 1.0 means it is ahead overall; 2.0 means it won twice what it lost. Below 1.0 is losing.
Slippage
The gap between the price it wanted and the price it actually got. Markets move in the split second an order takes to fill. Small negative slippage is normal and is a cost, like a fee.
bps (basis points)
A unit for very small percentages. 1 bps = 0.01%. So 100 bps = 1%. Used because 'we lost 0.08%' is harder to read than '8 bps'.
Leverage
Borrowing from the exchange to bet bigger than your money. At 2x, $5,000 controls $10,000 — which doubles the gains AND the losses. This system is capped well below what the exchange would allow.
Notional
The full size of a bet including borrowed money. A $500 notional position may only be using $250 of your own cash if leverage is 2x.
Circuit breaker
An automatic shut-off. If losses reach a set limit in a day or a week, it closes everything and stops trading. It cannot be overridden with a flag — clearing it is a deliberate human action that gets recorded.
Anchor
The account balance that the loss limit is measured from. It only ratchets upward: if the anchor fell as you lost, the 'limit' would keep retreating and would never actually stop anything.
Drawdown
How far the account has fallen from its highest point. The number that tells you how bad the worst stretch felt.
Strategy (rule)
The mechanical if-then that decides trades. No judgement, no AI — it reads prices and follows its condition exactly the same way every time.
Momentum
A rule that buys what is already going up, betting the move continues. It has no profit target — it rides the move until the move dies.
Mean reversion
A rule that buys what has dropped, betting it bounces back to normal. The opposite instinct to momentum.
Cycle
One round of 'look at the prices, decide, maybe trade'. It runs constantly. Most cycles correctly do nothing.
Hypothesis
A specific testable idea about what might predict prices — e.g. 'coins that jumped hard in the last 20 minutes keep rising'.
Out-of-sample
Testing an idea on data it has never seen. This is the honest test. Any idea can be tuned to look brilliant on data it was built from; only unseen data tells you whether it found something real.
Validated / rejected
Validated means the idea passed out-of-sample testing. Rejected means it failed. Almost everything gets rejected — that is the system working, not the system being broken.
UNVALIDATED
The rule currently placing trades has never passed that test. It runs to prove the plumbing works, not because there is evidence it makes money.
Sharpe ratio
Return compared to how bumpy the ride was. Higher is better. It penalises wild swings, so a smooth small gain can score better than a violent big one.
Ledger
The tamper-evident record of every experiment ever run, including the failed ones. Each entry is hash-chained to the previous, so edits to history show up.
Every number here is calculated by ordinary code from the trade record and the research ledger. No AI writes, edits, or reviews any figure on this page.
Practice results are not evidence and cannot promote an idea to real trading — ADR-0019 §4.
